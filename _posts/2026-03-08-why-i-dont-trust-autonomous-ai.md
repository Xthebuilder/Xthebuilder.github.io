# Why I Don't Trust Autonomous AI

I love AI but only when it listens to me. I don't really like how AI sometimes just gives an incorrect response and can hallucinate. In my latest blog post I dive into why me and autonomous AI don't get along.

## The Problem with Autonomous AI

I love AI and I love autonomy—what I don't like is the combination of those two technologies. AI is great when humans can watch over it and audit it at any point. Autonomy can be very dangerous to attach to AI for the simple fact that AI has a tendency to hallucinate. A hallucination can lead to an autonomous dump of system data, something that really couldn't happen without giving AI a loose leash.

## My Perspective on Responsible AI Use

I personally believe that AI is great when it's not given free reign. I believe the best way to responsibly use AI is to not be trusting at all with it. Make sure everything your agent does you can see when it's happening and maybe even why. AI is probabilistic and makes guesses—to attach that logic to something that can act on its own is a recipe for disaster in my opinion. It's like an architectural problem. If AI was exact and never hallucinated, I would think differently.

## Evidence: When Autonomous AI Goes Wrong

When the Replit agent destroyed data for 1200 executives and 1190 companies, it highlights what I'm talking about. I don't think that's acceptable and I think that's the issue of the engineer. AI is known to do things randomly and he still attached it to essential data. The fact that his instructions were ignored shows that it's just a problem that can't be fixed through simple prompting. It is also embarrassing to say that a tool meant to help out accidentally deleted massive amounts of data.

## Drawing the Line: I'm Not Anti-AI

I use AI everyday, so I'm not someone who is just anti-AI. Would I ever allow my agents to do things that I don't watch or can't check? Not with a ten foot pole. Why? Because in my year of using AI everyday I routinely catch Claude Code and realize okay I have to be essentially the handler for my agent. I think that's the best way to think of it because I deploy the agent but it's under my responsibility what it produces.

## What You Should Do

Think about AI as your digital assistant—you'd rarely let your assistant do many things that you couldn't at least check if you wanted to. Will you check everyday? Most likely not. I actually only check if something goes wrong, but I like to put strong guard rails around my AI because whatever it produces is still my responsibility.

---

*AI is a powerful tool, but it's just that—a tool. And like any tool, it needs a responsible operator at the controls.*
